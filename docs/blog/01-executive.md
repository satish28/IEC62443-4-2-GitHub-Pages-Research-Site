# Why Industrial Components Need Cybersecurity

## The Business Case for IEC 62443-4-2

**Audience:** Executive and technical management

Industrial cybersecurity discussions often begin with firewalls, segmentation and monitoring. Those controls matter, but they do not answer a fundamental question:

> **What happens if the component that performs the industrial function is itself misused or compromised?**

IEC 62443-4-2 brings cybersecurity requirements down to the component level.

This changes the management conversation from:

**“Is our network secure?”**

to:

**“Are the components performing our industrial functions designed to operate securely?”**

### The seven management questions

1. Who are you?
2. What are you allowed to do?
3. Can I trust what you are running?
4. What information must be protected?
5. Who can talk to you?
6. What happens when something goes wrong?
7. Can you keep operating?

### The RELAY-001 demonstrator

A simple relay is used as a teaching and engineering demonstrator. The goal is not certification; the goal is to make component security visible:

```text
Function → Security Requirement → Control → Test → Evidence
```

### Management takeaway

**Cybersecurity cannot stop at the firewall.**

A mature industrial cybersecurity strategy must consider the security properties of the components that ultimately perform the industrial function.
